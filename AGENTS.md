# Agent Guidelines: S&P 500 Top N Strategy

Quantitative backtesting engine and interactive Google Sheets dashboard for S&P 500 Top N ($N \in \{3, 5, 10\}$) strategies across 10y, 20y, and 30y horizons (1994–2024).

## Core Principles & Invariants
- **Zero External Dependencies**: Python 3 standard library only (`argparse`, `dataclasses`, `csv`, `json`, `math`, `unittest`). Do not introduce pandas, numpy, or third-party packages.
- **Unleveraged Cash Invariant**: `cash >= 0.0` at all times. Rebalancing must remain strictly self-financing without margin borrowing. Buys are clamped to available cash proceeds.
- **Dividend Accounting & Cash Pooling**: Split-adjusted cash dividends from `data/sp500_dividends.json` (annual) or `data/sp500_quarterly_dividends.json` (quarterly) are credited prior to rebalancing and pooled into available cash. Exact corporate action dates and amounts are used (never divided by 4).
- **Terminal Actions**: A constituent that stops trading is settled from `data/terminal_actions.json` before the period is valued, never sold at a market price it no longer has. Cash and delisting-to-zero are disposals; a stock conversion into a priceable acquirer is an IRC §368 reorganisation that carries FIFO basis forward and realises **no** gain, and one into an acquirer this repository cannot price is a disposal. Cash paid alongside stock is §356 boot. Index-removal dates are enforced in the dataset layer beside `EFFECTIVE_INCLUSION_DATES`, not in `engine/`. See `docs/DATA_PROVENANCE.md` §4.3.16.
- **Tax Model**: Decoupled dual tax settlement (dividend income taxed separately at $\tau$; capital loss carryforwards offset only realized capital gains, never dividend income). FIFO lot depletion, cumulative capital loss carryforwards, default 30.0% rate (`0.30`).
- **Rebalancing Frequencies**: Supports both Annual and Quarterly rebalancing. Q1–Q3 dynamically drift constituent weights by price performance relative to the index; Q4 re-anchors to official year-end factsheet weights.
- **Benchmark**: S&P 500 Total Return (`^SP500TR`) for pre-tax comparisons, and dynamic after-tax total return modeling (`^GSPC` price return + `^SP500TR` synthetic dividend yield with dividend taxation and terminal liquidation tax) for after-tax comparisons.
- **Pricing & Dividends**: Vendor-sourced prices (`data/sp500_prices.json`, `data/sp500_quarterly_prices.json`) and dividends per share (`data/sp500_dividends.json`, `data/sp500_quarterly_dividends.json`) are split-adjusted to 2024-12-31. The constituents priced from fund schedules are the exception at **both** ends: their prices are adjusted to each registrant's **own final observation** rather than to the present (a delisted registrant has no subsequent corporate actions, so adjustment to today is undefined), and their dividends are converted into that same basis by `scripts/derive_issuer_dividend_series.py`. A dividend and a price for one of these constituents are comparable to each other and **not** to `data/raw/tickers/`. See `docs/DATA_PROVENANCE.md` §4.1, §4.3.12 and §4.3.15. **A split record must be complete, and completeness is checked across filings, not against prices**: comparing a derived price to the issuer's reported band applies the recorded factor to both sides, so a missing split cancels out and the band still passes. Two filings reporting the same quarter disagree by exactly the corporate actions between them, which is the check that catches it (§4.3.20).
- **Sourced Zero Versus Unknown**: an empty dividend series means *unknown*; an explicit `0.0` means a filing states the registrant never paid, and the sentence and accession are recorded in `data/raw/ground_truth/derived_dividend_series.json`. These are different claims and must not be collapsed. A never-paid statement is bounded by the date it was filed and is void if a later filing of the same registrant reports a payment — Viacom filed such statements for eight years and then began paying in 2003-Q3.
- **Risk & Drawdown Measurement**: Max Drawdown is measured across discrete periodic observation dates (annual year-end or quarterly quarter-end valuations), reflecting endpoint rebalance valuations rather than continuous daily extremes.
- **Published Figures Must Earn Their Place, Then Survive a Rot Test**: before anything else, ask whether the figure belongs at all. **A figure earns its place only when the explanation collapses without it.** Deleting one removes the maintenance chore permanently; asserting one merely relocates it. What fails this gate is the downstream consequence — a number describing how a data change moved portfolio performance, where the reader needs the direction and not the magnitude. A table that has acquired a footnote saying its figures have since moved has already failed it. Once a figure has earned its place it is one of three things, and the question that sorts them is **can a reader verify this without running our code?** **Sourced** — read from a filing — cannot rot, because the filing cannot change; it carries a `source_ref` (an accession the archive holds, a repo-relative path that resolves, or an explicit `unarchived:` marker naming what is missing) so the filing can actually be found, and `scripts/verify_provenance.py` re-checks it. **A resolving `source_ref` proves the pointer is good, not that the figure is in the document it names** — that is what `verify_provenance.py` is for. The marker is the form that carries the weight: it lets an honest gap through while making it visible and countable, the same move `refused_filings` and the sourced-zero rule already make, and `python3 scripts/audit_doc_figures.py --source-refs` prints the census and every gap. **Self-checking** — the document prints the operation and every input reaches an immutable leaf — needs no test, because the check is published. §4.6.4's spinoff proceeds are the archetype: `$0.324084 \times \$45.875 = \$14.86735`, a Form 8937 ratio times a stated close, with operands, operation and result all on the page. **A leaf is an immutable input, not necessarily a filing**: a filing, an archived response under `data/raw/tickers/`, or a market datum the document states directly. Never anything our pipeline computes. **The trace is recursive and the surface form lies**: §4.3.6's `89.8% (494/550)` prints an operation too, but `494` is our own match count, so it rots. The recursion crosses sections — a figure computed from a rot-exposed figure is rot-exposed, wherever that input is documented. Note also that an immutable leaf can still be *unsourced*, which is a provenance defect and a different question from rot. **Pipeline** — anything else the code produces, from row counts to CAGRs — rots on any data or engine change, and may be published *only if a test asserts it*. If you will not assert it, state the claim qualitatively and point at the script that prints the magnitude. Assert the **claim, not the cell value**: pin invariants exactly (a market-cap book cannot see past the pool cut), signs and thresholds with headroom (momentum at Top 10 is negative in all six cells; the survivorship effect stays under 0.5pp). A `guard` in the manifest means *a test asserts the claim this figure evidences*, not that a test pins this value. Pinning every published value relocates the maintenance chore instead of removing it — a legitimate data improvement should move a table, not fail twenty assertions. Tables of computed figures carry the command that regenerates them. `docs/doc_figure_manifest.json` records the verdict for every published figure and `tests/test_doc_figures.py` fails when a new one is added without one; note that the ratchet sees figures, not claims, so a stale assertion phrased as a date range is invisible to it, and `inputs_trace` is prose, so the recursion above cannot be machine-checked either. **`README.md` is stricter still: no computed figure at all, asserted or not.** It states the claim qualitatively — *never hurt*, *hurt on average*, *a minority are corroborated* — and points at the `docs/DATA_PROVENANCE.md` section holding the magnitude, because the README is the copy nobody remembers to update. When a published figure is found stale, the fix is to **remove it, not refresh it**; refreshing relocates the chore to the next data change.
- **Token & Diff Efficiency**: Large exports (`outputs/*.csv`, `scripts/google_apps_script.js`) and raw archives (`data/raw/`) are marked `-diff` in `.gitattributes` and excluded in `.ignore`. Always run `python3 run_backtest.py --no-export` for testing. When reviewing diffs, use `git diff -- . ':!outputs/' ':!scripts/google_apps_script.js'`. To inspect intentional diffs on datasets, use `git diff --text data/`. `data/raw/` filings can exceed 500KB each; when one must be inspected, use targeted extraction (`grep -A N` or a limited read range) rather than reading the full file.

## Architecture
See `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` for full component diagrams and schema details.
- `engine/models.py`: Domain dataclasses (`ConstituentSnapshot`, `HoldingTarget`, `TaxLot`, `TradeOrder`, `AnnualLedgerEntry`, `StrategyResult`).
- `engine/tax_lots.py`: `FIFOTaxLotManager` (FIFO queues, lot splitting, capital gains tax settlement, loss carryforwards, unrealized gains).
- `engine/selector.py`: `BaseSelector`, `MarketCapSelector`, `PerformanceSelector` ($w_i = W_i / \sum_{j=1}^N W_j$).
- `engine/data_loader.py`: Point-in-time constituent datasets, split-adjusted prices, and split-adjusted dividend history (1994–2024).
- `engine/backtest.py`: `PortfolioSimulator` implementing dividend cash pooling and two-phase rebalancing (pre-tax valuation, exits/trims, decoupled dual tax, secondary trims, cash-clamped buys).
- `engine/benchmarks.py`: Benchmark compounding and metrics calculation across SPX, MSCI World, FBGRX, and Nasdaq 100 (`BenchmarkSuite`, `compute_all_benchmarks`).
- `engine/metrics.py`: Math functions (`calculate_cagr`, `calculate_cumulative_return`, `calculate_max_drawdown`, `calculate_turnover`, `calculate_tax_drag`, `calculate_alpha`, `calculate_terminal_metrics`, `calculate_benchmark_annual_series`).
- `engine/scenarios.py`: Multi-tier scenario matrix builder across tax tiers, frequencies, and horizons.
- `engine/terminal_view.py`: Terminal presentation utilities (`format_terminal_table`, `build_strategy_row`, `build_benchmark_row`).
- `engine/templates/`: Modular Google Apps Script dashboard templates (`gas/00_` to `05_`).
- `engine/exporters/`: CSV report writers, Google Apps Script generator, and pipeline orchestrator.
- `run_backtest.py`: Decomposed primary CLI runner and pipeline orchestrator (`--frequency`, `--compare-frequencies`, `--no-export`, `--benchmark`).

## Common Commands
- Run backtest (fast, no file exports): `python3 run_backtest.py --no-export`
- Run backtest & regenerate full exports: `python3 run_backtest.py --compare-frequencies`
- Run quarterly backtest: `python3 run_backtest.py --frequency quarterly --no-export`
- Token-lean review diff: `git diff -- . ':!outputs/' ':!scripts/google_apps_script.js'`
- Run full test suite: `python3 -m unittest discover tests`
- Run quarterly test suite: `python3 -m unittest tests/test_quarterly.py`
